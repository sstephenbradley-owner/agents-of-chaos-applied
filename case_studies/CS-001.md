**Evidence status:** documented run with data. Per-round, per-validator scores exist in the primary session transcript. The published write-ups (method paper draft, case-study inventory) carry placeholders or averages instead of these figures.

Notes on the scores:

- Round 1 actually produced three reports, not two. Chat A reviewed the present-day financial research (4/10). Chat B reviewed the historical-parallel research (3/10). Chat A then also reviewed the historical-parallel research (5/10). The drafter's final table used 4 and 3; the 5/10 report does not appear in any later summary.
- The target changed between rounds. Round 1 scored two separate research drafts. Rounds 2 and 3 scored the chapter argument as revised by the author's counter-arguments. The scores therefore measure the argument as it evolved, not one fixed document.
- The case-study inventory gives Round 2 as "approximately 6.0". The transcript supports 6.5.
- The method paper's Figure 1 and Section 5.1 describe "endpoint averages with interpolated intermediate values". The real intermediate values above were in the transcript the whole time.

## Error categories found

The method paper lists five error categories for this run. The table below shows where each one actually appeared.

| Category (paper's term) | What the record shows | Found by | Round | In original draft? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial instrument misclassification | A government loan facility described as a procurement contract | Chat A | 1 | Yes |
| Historical chronology error | Claim that "upstream" policy capture was new in 2026, when early 20th-century banks had lobbied for interventions directly | Both chats | 1 | Yes |
| Legislative timeline misattribution | A 2025 federal ban on foreign drone components attributed to the current administration's war planning; the ban was a statutory mandate from the 2024 NDAA | Chat B only | 2 | No. The claim entered in a Round 2 counter-argument, suggested by the author and written up by the drafter |
| Authority overreach (the paper uses a different label for this) | An outside analyst borrowing an insider's confessional voice | Both chats | 1 | Yes |
| Scale-metric conflation | Stock price appreciation of a loss-making micro-cap company compared with realized wartime corporate profits | Both chats (drafter summary: "unanimous CRITICAL") | 1 | Yes |

Other errors recorded in the session but not in the paper's five categories:

- **Securities-backed credit argument (Round 2).** A counter-argument claimed the "buy, borrow, die" strategy applied to the micro-cap holding. Both chats rejected it: banks do not lend on favorable terms against volatile, loss-making micro-cap stock. Chat B rated it 3/10 and called it CRITICAL. This argument was also introduced in Round 2, at the author's suggestion.
- **Historical profit figure.** The draft cited a steel company's wartime profit rise ($105M to $240M) to Senate Document No. 259. Chat A said the Senate document records $888,931,000, and that the smaller figures were the pamphlet's rhetorical rounding. The drafter accepted this. The session contains no independent check of the $888.9M figure.
- **Text conflation.** A famous confessional passage was presented as part of the 1935 pamphlet. Chat A pointed out it was first published in a magazine essay the same year.
- **Omission.** Chat A rated as CRITICAL the draft's failure to mention civilian casualties at all.
- **Source quality.** Encyclopedia and secondary citations were flagged for replacement with primary filings.

## Fabrication attempts

None recorded by either validator in this run. Two drafter source errors (the steel-profit attribution and the text conflation) are logged in `fabrication_log.csv` as misattributions.

## What changed in the protocol

- This run became the first formal written example of the cross-model workflow and prompted the decision, the same evening, to write up the method for publication.
- The Round 3 reports from both chats stated that a single-model review would have kept the errors. The method paper quotes these statements as support for the approach.
- No protocol version change is tied to this run. The v2.0 changes came from CS-002.

## Limitations

- The scores are self-reported by each validator against its own reading of a 1 to 10 scale. No rubric, anchor examples, or inter-rater check existed at the time.
- The validators were two chats of one model family. The only asymmetry was context, not model family.
- One of the five error categories (legislative timeline), and the securities-backed credit error outside those categories, were introduced during the counter-argument rounds. They show the protocol catching errors added mid-process, not errors in the original draft.
- The method paper says both validators found all five categories in Round 1. The transcript does not support that: the legislative timeline error was found in Round 2 by one chat.
- The replacement figure for the steel profits came from the validator and was accepted without an independent check.
- The Gemini reports were uploaded as PDFs or pasted text. The PDF contents are not preserved in the chat export, so some Round 1 detail survives only through the drafter's summaries.
